How it works
  1. Group stage: 12 groups of 4; every team plays 3 games.
  2. Into the Round of 32: the top 2 of each group (24 teams) plus the 8 best third-placed teams of the 12 go through.
  3. Knockouts: single elimination from there. Round of 16 → Quarter-finals → Semi-finals → Final.
Hosts & key dates

Played across the USA, Canada & Mexico. It kicks off June 11 at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City (the first ground to host three World Cups) and ends with the final on July 19 at MetLife Stadium, New York/New Jersey.

About this project

A free, open fan-made companion for the 2026 World Cup: all 104 matches in your timezone, live, with no ads and no sign-up.

Why you'd use it
  • All 104 matches in your timezone, with a live countdown and a one-tap calendar export, so a kickoff never sneaks past you
  • Browse all 48 nations: squads (tap a player for their profile — photo, age, height, weight, club, caps and match log), form, matches and a playing-style read for each, and make one your favourite so the whole app takes on its colours and projected route to the final
  • Compare any two players or any two teams head-to-head — form, strength, playing style and pedigree, with the model's odds if the two teams met
  • Every match carries a win probability and a predicted scoreline from a transparent model, which flags the factors moving the call when they stand out
  • Open any match for the full picture, live or finished: a team-coloured scoreboard that refreshes in place, the win probability leading while it's live, match stars with full-size player shots, live commentary, the goal timeline, line-ups on a formation pitch, stats, reports and FIFA's official deep analysis (expected goals, distance covered, heatmaps)
  • A full knockout predictor: see the live projected Round of 32 "as it stands" up top, then order the groups, pick the best third-placed teams, and play your way to a champion across up to three saved scenarios, each with its own path to glory
  • The whole qualification picture and tournament stats: the Tables tab with live group tables, the best-third race and a projected Round of 32; the Golden Boot and team leaderboards; all-time World Cup records (tracked live as 2026 players close in); and the full FIFA World Ranking
  • The latest headlines from football desks worldwide, plus install as an app, offline support, light & dark, and a quick search (⌘K) for any team, player or match
The prediction model

Win probabilities come from a Dixon-Coles scoring model. Each side carries a strength rating: an Elo that updates after every result and leans on official expected goals, not just the final score. That gets refined by an attack-versus-defence read of how the two teams actually play, by home advantage for the host nations, and, late in the groups, by what each side still needs to qualify. It's tuned to football's history, never to this tournament's own results, and it surfaces what's behind a call when there's a factor worth flagging.

How it works

It's a single static page with no app server. Scores and data are refreshed by automated jobs that publish plain JSON the page reads, so your browser never calls a sports API directly and no keys are ever exposed. Kickoff times convert to your device's clock automatically.

Your privacy

No tracking, no cookies, no accounts. Analytics are off by default. Your team, timezone and saved predictions stay on your device.

How this was built the technical story▾
No build step

The whole app is one index.html, one styles.css and one app.js — plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no bundler, no server. Edit a file, refresh the page. It loads as a single static page from a CDN, so it's quick anywhere and trivial to host.

The data pipeline

GitHub Actions do the fetching, not your browser. On a schedule, jobs pull from public sources — openfootball for the schedule, FIFA and ESPN for live scores, line-ups, stats and commentary, FIFA's post-match Enhanced Football Intelligence PDFs for the deep analysis — and bake the results into plain JSON committed to the repo. The page only ever reads that JSON, so no sports API is ever called from your device and no keys are exposed.

Built to work offline

A service worker caches the shell so it keeps working on a plane or a dodgy connection, while staying network-first for code and scores — so you get fresh data when you're online and never get stuck on a stale build.

Built in the open

Shipped iteratively, one small build at a time — you're on build —. Written by hand with an AI pair-programmer, every change reviewed and verified in a live preview before it went out.
